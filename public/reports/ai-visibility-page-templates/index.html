What AI Visibility Actually Looks Like on a Page

A successful AI visibility strategy is rooted in audience intelligence and category research, an in-depth prompt audit (of relevant prompts), and a consistent and strategic content engine. Once you get to the step where you're putting words on the page that you want to be cited or used for the recommendation, it turns into more of a checklist — FAQ schema, one topic per page, lead every section with a direct answer.

When I work with clients and agency teams, the easiest way to showcase this piece is a visual mockup. I actually provide page templates outlined like the below for every category page, product line, etc. to ensure the implementation is successful.

The companies referenced here are fictional, for educational purposes, and cover a few of the brand types I work with — medical/healthcare, CPG with retail distribution, and local consumer services. As the AI-optimized page templates showcase, the on-page priorities are almost entirely different based on the company's goals.

Page notes
1
AI matches one page to one questionOne topic, one intent, in the H1. The page is named for the comparison a clinician actually types. A page called "Cardiac Monitoring Solutions" covering the whole portfolio answers no single question well enough to be quoted.
2
AI search looks for trust and authority signalsA named reviewer with a credential anyone can check. MD, board certification, job title. A device company already has a physician check this content for accuracy, and that name usually stays in an internal file. On the page it gives a reader and an engine a person to attribute the answer to — same as a CPA on a tax page or a licensed electrician on a wiring page.
3
AI favors pages it can tell are currentPublish date and clinical-review date, both visible. Dating the clinical review does double duty: it is the freshness signal the checklist asks for, and it is the audit trail the category requires.
4
AI lifts the clearest self-contained passage it can findThe answer block. About 80 words, above everything else, written to be lifted whole. It answers the comparison in specifics — duration, form factor, and the clinical reason duration matters — and it says plainly where the older modality is still the right order. That last sentence is what makes the page quotable rather than promotional.
5
AI matches the buyer's question to a headingSection headings written as the questions buyers ask, each followed immediately by its answer. The heading is the retrieval hook; the first sentence under it is the extractable unit.
6
AI pulls facts from table cells more cleanly than from proseThe comparison table. Four rows a clinician can lift, in cells with units. Naming the competing modality is fine; naming a competing manufacturer usually is not, which is why the column header is a device category.
7
AI cites whoever published the numberOriginal data with a stated method, kept operational. Wear-completion and turnaround come from fulfillment records, so they publish without a clinical-claim review. Diagnostic yield is a clinical claim and belongs in the literature. It puts a proprietary number on the page in the part of the dataset that clears review fastest.
8
AI reads a question-and-answer pair as a ready-made answerThe FAQ block, with FAQPage markup underneath. The reimbursement question is on the page because clinicians ask it constantly; the answer text comes from the reimbursement team, and the page links to the current coding guidance rather than restating a code that changes annually.
9
AI needs corroborating records to treat a company as a known entityThe entity block. One consistent name, address, and phone, plus the outbound links that let an engine confirm the company exists. For a device manufacturer, the FDA device listing is the strongest third-party record available — a public database entry tying the company name to the cleared device.
Claims review shapes every number on this page. Each figure in the answer block, the comparison table, and the FAQ traces to cleared labeling, published data, or an operational record with a stated method. A marketing team cannot round a number up to make the sentence land better, and the page still has to answer the question in specifics. Vague copy loses the citation, so the work is getting precise language through review.

robots.txt, at the root of the domain. Four lines that decide whether the site is eligible at all.

A blocked crawler is invisible in reporting. The pages keep ranking in Google, no AI answer ever cites them, and analytics shows nothing that explains it.

FAQPage markup, in a script tag on the page, matching the visible Q&A word for word.

Article markup carries the reviewer and the dates in machine-readable form. The byline on screen and the markup in code have to agree. reviewedBy is the property that expresses clinical review.
{ "@context": "https://schema.org", "@type": "Article", "headline": "Patch Cardiac Monitoring vs. a Holter Monitor", "datePublished": "2026-03-04", "dateModified": "2026-08-19", "reviewedBy": { "@type": "Person", "name": "Dana Whitfield", "honorificSuffix": "MD, FACC", "jobTitle": "Medical Director", "hasCredential": "Board certified, cardiac electrophysiology", "knowsAbout": ["ambulatory cardiac monitoring", "arrhythmia detection"] }, "publisher": { "@type": "Organization", "name": "Solvane Medical" } }
Organization markup carries the company's identity. sameAs is the list of independent places that confirm the company is real. Schema.org also defines a MedicalDevice type for the device itself, which describes the product for machines that read it even though no search engine renders anything from it today.

Off-site work
Literature and trade pressPeer-reviewed publications, conference abstracts, and clinical trade media. The heaviest citation weight available, and the only place clinical performance claims belong.
Regulatory recordsThe FDA device listing. A public database tying the company to the cleared device, which is a stronger identity record than any commercial directory.
WikidataAn entry with the company's founding date, location, and industry, linked from the site's markup.
ResearchThe operational dataset, published as a page other people cite, with the clinical questions answered in the literature.
What carries this page: a device company that wants to be cited is competing on verifiable precision. The named clinical reviewer, the published literature, and the regulatory record do most of the work; the schema makes them legible to a machine.

Page notes
1
AI matches one page to one questionOne product, one page. The product page answers "does this specific faucet fit my sink." A separate page answers "which faucet is best for a farmhouse sink," and links to this one. Two intents, two pages.
2
AI favors facts it can see were verified recentlyA verified-on date for the specs. On a product page the freshness signal that matters is whether the specs and price are current, so date the verification rather than the marketing copy.
3
AI quotes the passage that answers the query in specificsThe answer block, written to the recommendation question. It leads with fit and dimensions because that is what the query is really asking. A spout height that clears an apron front is the detail an answer quotes.
4
An agent transacts on price, stock, and seller dataRetailer availability, on the page and in the markup. Four named retailers, each with a price and a stock state. This is the agentic-commerce layer — an assistant that goes from naming a product to adding it to a cart acts on the price, stock, and seller it can read. Product schema carries all three in code.
5
AI compares products on values it can read from a cellA real specification table. Numbers in table cells with labeled units. The same facts written into a paragraph of body copy are far harder to extract cleanly.
6
AI reads an on-page Q&A as an answer already matched to its questionThe FAQ block, answering fit and install questions, with FAQPage markup underneath.
7
AI answers a comparison question with whatever comparison it can findAn in-line comparison across their own models. The buyer's question is comparative, and this answers it using only facts the brand owns. Naming competitors is usually off the table for a manufacturer; third-party reviewers handle that side.
8
AI needs one consistent set of facts across every sourceThe entity block. For a brand in four retail catalogs, the risk is conflicting data — three spellings of the model name and two different spout heights across retailer listings. Consistency across those listings is the work.
Schema
Product markup. The offers array is the piece a shopping-oriented answer reads. Price, currency, availability, and the named seller for each retailer.

The same discipline applies to the data the brand sends its retailers. A spec sheet that says "16.5″" on the brand site and "16 1/2 inch" in a retailer feed reads as two different products.

Off-site work
CommunityThe subreddit threads where people ask which faucet fits a farmhouse sink. Named, useful answers; promotional posts get removed.
Review pressIndependent reviews and roundups. These are where a named head-to-head against a competitor can happen.
Retailer listingsFour retailer product pages carrying the brand's specs. Cited often, and controlled through the product feed rather than directly.
WikidataThe brand as a known entity, so "Cedarline" resolves to a company rather than a word.
What carries this page: a brand that wants to be recommended is competing on fit and availability. Extractable specs and readable retailer offers do most of the work, and third-party reviews supply the corroboration the brand's own page cannot.

Page notes
1
AI answers location questions with location-specific pagesOne service, one city, in the H1. The query has two variables and the page answers both. The failure mode here is forty near-identical city pages with the town name swapped — each one needs its own permit rules, pricing, service-area list, and local reviews, or it adds nothing.
2
AI weighs who stands behind the claimA named, certified technician on the page. Certification, state license number, and years on the job. On a consumer-service page this is the trust signal that carries the most weight, and it costs nothing beyond adding the byline.
3
AI needs to see when the price was last checkedA pricing-verified date. Price is the whole query, so the date is what makes the range safe to quote and gives the team a set cadence for keeping it current.
4
AI can quote your price only if your page publishes oneThe answer block, with an actual number in it. Publishing price is the hardest ask on this page, and most brands resist it. The number reaches the buyer either way — from a competitor who published a range, or from a Reddit thread by a customer who felt overcharged. A range plus the three factors that move it puts the sourced version on your own page.
5
AI answers the follow-up question from the rows of a tableThe price factors as a table. The follow-up question after "how much" is "why does it vary," and a table answers it in cells an engine can lift row by row.
6
AI reads ratings that carry a source and a countReviews, with a count, a date, and the platforms named. A star rating with no source and no date is decoration. Note the markup limit below — review markup on your own site has rules.
7
AI pulls the permit and timeline answers straight from the Q&AThe FAQ block, answering the permit, timeline, and financing questions, with FAQPage markup underneath.
8
AI needs to know which city a page coversThe service-area list and the links to the other metros. This is what tells an engine which "Columbus" and which suburbs, and it keeps three city pages reading as one company instead of three thin duplicates.
9
AI builds local answers from the business profileName, address, phone, and hours — identical to the Google Business Profile. Local AI answers and AI Overviews pull from the profile directly, so a mismatch between it and the page is a conflict the engine resolves by trusting neither.
Schema
LocalBusiness markup. HVACBusiness is a real schema.org subtype of LocalBusiness. areaServed and openingHoursSpecification are the properties that answer "near me" and "open now."

The price range, in markup. Service with an offers block carries the number the query is asking for. minPrice and maxPrice are the properties that make a range machine-readable.

One limit worth knowing on reviews. Schema.org defines AggregateRating, and Google's structured-data guidelines treat reviews a business collects and publishes about itself as self-serving, so they do not earn review rich results. Displaying the rating with its source and count still helps a reader and gives an engine something to read. The rating that carries weight sits on the review platforms themselves.
Off-site work
Business profileThe Google Business Profile, complete and current, for each location. Top of the list for this business.
Review platformsGoogle, Yelp, Angi, Nextdoor. Volume, recency, and owner responses all read.
CommunityLocal subreddits and neighborhood groups, where "who did you use for your furnace" gets asked constantly.
Local pressLocal business journals and "best of" lists carry weight for a location-bound service.
What carries this page: a local service is competing on proximity, price transparency, and reviews. The business profile and the review platforms carry the answer, and the page's job is to publish a real number and agree with the profile in every detail.

The on-page checklist takes ten minutes to read. Deciding which parts carry a given business takes judgment, and it depends on what that business needs the answer to do. B2B and B2C is the shorthand people use, and it splits the wrong way — a consumer brand at retail and a consumer service company have almost nothing in common here, while the medtech company and the local service both live or die on a named credential.
Additional notes
FAQ schemaThe markup still helps a machine parse a question and its answer. The other half: Google restricted FAQ rich results to government and health sites in 2023, so FAQPage markup produces nothing visible in ordinary search results. Add it for extraction and set expectations accordingly.
Entity schema has three flavorsOrganization for the device manufacturer, Brand plus Product for the retail brand, LocalBusiness for the service company. Same checklist item, three different schema types, and picking the wrong one wastes the work.
Who owns each pieceThe work splits four ways — what a visitor sees, code a visitor never sees, activity off the site, and measurement. Only the first belongs to page design, which is why the rest tends to land with whoever has time rather than whoever owns it.

All three brands are fictional. Every specification, price, figure, and license number shown is illustrative and exists to demonstrate the pattern. Schema examples use current schema.org types and properties.
