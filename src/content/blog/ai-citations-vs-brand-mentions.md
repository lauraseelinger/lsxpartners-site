I was going through a client's monthly report and their AI mentions had dropped 26%. On its own, that's the kind of number that makes a client think "wait — what am I paying you for?" But right next to it, their citations had gone up.

A mention is when AI names your brand in its answer. A citation is when AI uses your content as the source — quoting it, linking it, pulling the answer straight from your page. Mentions falling while citations climb isn't a loss of visibility. It's a consolidation toward the signal that actually matters — the difference between AI knowing you exist and AI trusting you enough to build its answer on you.

That's why you can't read the mentions number alone. For that client, mentions were only one line on the page — citations and cited pages had both gone *up*, for the second month running. What looked like a decline was AI name-dropping the brand less and citing it as a source more. A mention gets you on the radar; being cited means the model read your stuff, decided it was worth using, and made you part of the answer. You want both, but citations are what move the needle.

## What's the difference between an AI mention and an AI citation?

A mention is a name-drop. You ask ChatGPT about running shoes and it says "brands like Brooks, Hoka, and New Balance are popular." Those brands got named. Nobody's content got cited — the model pulled the names from memory.

A citation is when the model goes and reads a page and uses it — quotes it, footnotes it, or clearly builds the answer from it. That page got cited. It's the source.

The reason the gap matters: mentions and citations come from different places. Mentions often come from the model's training data — what it already "knows" about you. Citations come from what the model retrieves and reads in the moment, which is the part you can actually influence.

## Why fewer AI mentions can be good news

Stop reading at the mentions line and you'd think visibility was slipping. Read the citations right next to it and the opposite was true.

Early on, AI would name the brand in a list of options and move on. As the work compounded — clearer answer-first content, getting the brand into the sources AI actually reads — the model started pulling from its pages as *the* source instead of listing it as one of many. A citation is the higher-value event: it's an authority signal and a path back to you. A bare mention is neither.

So the mentions number sliding was a symptom of the citations number rising. Look at either one alone and you'd read the situation backwards — which is exactly why [one AI visibility score can't tell you the story](/blog/what-your-ai-visibility-score-cant-tell-you/).

## Which one actually matters — mentions or citations?

Both — they just do different jobs.

Mentions build familiarity. If AI keeps naming you when someone explores a category, you're in the consideration set, and that matters, especially early. But a mention is passive. The model said your name; it didn't vouch for you.

A citation is AI putting weight on you. It read your content, decided it was worth using, and made you part of the answer. That's the closest thing to a recommendation, and it's the one that tends to show up when someone's further down the path to actually buying.

If I have to prioritize for a client, I go citations every time. A brand that's cited is a brand AI trusts, and that trust is what turns "here are some options" into "here's who you should look at."

## How do you get cited by AI instead of just mentioned?

You earn citations the way you earn any source's trust — by being genuinely useful and easy to pull from. A few things that move it:

- **Answer the actual question, clearly and up front.** AI cites content that gives a clean, liftable answer, not three paragraphs of warm-up. Say the answer, then explain it.
- **Bring something only you have.** Original data, a real point of view, a specific example. Models won't cite the tenth page that paraphrases the other nine — they cite the one that adds something.
- **Show up where AI reads.** A lot of citations come from third-party sources AI trusts — Reddit, YouTube, industry publications — not just your homepage. ([Here's how I audit where you stand](/blog/how-to-audit-ai-visibility).)
- **Make it structurally easy to extract** — real headers, clear answers under them, the kind of clean structure a model can quote without guessing.

None of that is a hack. It's being the most useful, most specific source on the question, now judged by a model instead of a person.
